# Literature survey

Nine peer-reviewed sources on transformer- and CNN-based change detection, Swin Transformer architectures,
bitemporal fusion and change-detection datasets. Titles, authors, venues, years and DOIs were verified against
the Crossref API on 11 November 2026. Method / strength / limitation summaries are the author's reading of each
paper; no numerical result from any paper is quoted.

## Problem statement

Urban planners, environmental agencies and disaster-response teams need to know where land use has changed between two satellite acquisitions, but comparing image pairs by eye does not scale and classical pixel differencing is swamped by illumination, seasonal and registration differences. Convolutional change-detection networks improved on this, yet their local receptive fields limit the long-range context needed to separate real structural change from such pseudo-change. This project designs, implements and evaluates a Siamese Swin Transformer that predicts pixel-level building/urban change from bitemporal imagery, benchmarks it against a CNN baseline (FC-Siam-diff) on the same held-out data, and delivers it as a working application (TerraVision AI) so an analyst can upload or fetch an image pair and obtain a verifiable change map.

## Research gaps

### G1 - Hierarchical transformers remain heavy for commodity hardware

Pure-transformer designs such as SwinSUNet (Zhang et al., 2022) and ChangeFormer (Bandara & Patel, 2022) report strong accuracy but use transformer decoders, long schedules and large-memory GPUs; BIT (Chen et al., 2022) is light but keeps a CNN backbone and a single-scale transformer. There is little evidence on how far a hierarchical Swin encoder can go when it must train and run inside 8 GB of VRAM.

**Status:** ADDRESSED in Phase 1: Swin-V2-Tiny encoder with a convolutional FPN decoder, mixed precision and sliding-window inference, trained and benchmarked on an RTX 5050 (8 GB).

### G2 - Benchmarks are narrow in geography, sensor and change type

LEVIR-CD (Chen & Shi, 2020) covers building change in Texas at 0.5 m; OSCD (Daudt et al., 2018) covers urban change at 10-60 m with only 24 city pairs. Models are rarely evaluated across sensors, resolutions or regions, so reported scores say little about transfer to, for example, Sentinel-2 over Indian cities.

**Status:** PARTLY ADDRESSED in Phase 1: the domain gap is measured (LEVIR-CD-only models score F1 of about 0-1% on the OSCD Sentinel-2 test cities) and closed in part by fine-tuning on OSCD RGB with radiometry matched to the application's imagery source. Multispectral input, more regions and multi-class change remain for Phase 2.

### G3 - Research models are not delivered as verifiable, geospatially correct tools

The surveyed papers publish architectures and scores on pre-cut patches. They do not address what an analyst needs in practice: validation of arbitrary input pairs, CRS-aware alignment, inference on full rasters, georeferenced outputs, honest reporting when ground truth is absent, and traceability of which checkpoint produced a result.

**Status:** ADDRESSED in Phase 1: TerraVision AI wraps both models in a tested application with input validation, reprojection, GeoTIFF/GeoJSON export, geodesic area and checkpoint versioning.

## Research objectives

1. Implement a Siamese Swin Transformer change-detection network with a trained decoder (not just a pre-trained backbone) that fits an 8 GB consumer GPU.
2. Implement and train a CNN baseline (FC-Siam-diff) and evaluate both models on the identical held-out LEVIR-CD test split with the same metrics and threshold.
3. Make the models work on real Sentinel-2 imagery: fine-tune both on OSCD (10 m) and measure the domain shift between 0.5 m aerial and 10 m satellite imagery on held-out test cities.
4. Build a reproducible data pipeline (verified download, leakage-free tiling, split manifest) and a configurable training / evaluation pipeline that logs every experiment in machine-readable form.
5. Deliver an end-to-end application: validated upload or satellite retrieval, background inference, interactive visualisation, ground-truth metrics, georeferenced export and persistent history.
6. Verify the system with automated tests that include boundary and negative cases, and document bugs with root causes.
